## Editorial checks for every new or adapted item

1. Read the entire completed text, including each accepted alternative; check subject, tense, spelling, pronoun placement, collocation, causality and reference time.
2. Ask whether a rejected form is actually a defensible alternative viewpoint. Credit a natural alternative with its own interpretation; do not accept a tense mechanically just because a grammatically possible sentence could be imagined elsewhere.
3. For connector choices, try every option in the complete discourse. If two options fit equally well, accept both with distinct feedback, revise the options, or make the instruction explicitly about the intended relationship. “Best” is not a licence for an arbitrary key.
4. Explain why the particular form fits this context in one or two lines. Feedback should name the relevant perspective or discourse relationship; conjugation feedback must identify the subject/person when useful.
5. Keep the imperfect/preterite contrast central. Separate optional commands, present and perfect forms, and retain their separate scoring where the UI promises it.
6. Avoid false shortcuts: imperfect ≠ unfinished now, preterite ≠ brief, `cuando/mientras/ayer` ≠ a forced tense, progressive ≠ imperfect by default, and preterite progressive ≠ completed object.
7. Protect the existing behaviour: blank answers can be checked and receive zero; accent-only errors receive half; natural alternatives receive full credit; first and best marks stay distinct; alternative readings stay visible.
8. Ensure all guide/practice links resolve, every new gap has feedback and a unique ID, counts and progress denominators are derived from the current catalogue, and existing students do not lose unrelated progress.
